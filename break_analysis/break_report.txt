====================================================================
BREAK ANALYSIS - was the 2004 rainfall jump real?
====================================================================
Loaded 883,128 daily records, 62 districts.

Background (documentation checked online):
  - The Kaggle page says the data come from NASA's POWER project, extracted for 93
    weather-station locations in 62 districts. That is MODELLED data, not station
    measurements, so changes in the underlying product can create artificial jumps.
  - An Open Data Nepal listing with the same date range and the same total row count
    (2 x 441,564 = 883,128) carries a note that precipitation is reported differently
    before and after January 2018. That is not 2004, so it does not explain this jump.
  - No documentation found for a change in 2004. The cause is UNVERIFIED.

CHECK 4 - Is the physics consistent? (1981-2003 vs 2005-2019)
                  1981-2003  2005-2019
corr_rain_RH           0.42       0.43

monsoon_RH            73.70      80.62
monsoon_humidity      14.06      15.06
  The rain-humidity correlation is similar in both periods, but the LEVELS of rainfall
  and humidity both stepped up. Real climate shifts and product changes can both do this,
  so this check cannot settle the question.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS
  - The jump is abrupt (within months), nearly simultaneous in almost every district,
    and appears in several moisture-related variables together, while mean temperature
    and pressure show no matching step.
  - That pattern is typical of a change in how a modelled dataset was produced, and it
    is difficult to attribute to natural climate variability. It is NOT proof: independent station
    records (e.g. from Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology) would be needed.
  - Suggested wording: 'The data contain an abrupt, nationwide step change in rainfall
    and humidity in early 2004, most likely a data-product artifact.'

Note: p-values come from a permutation test that treats years as independent, so they
are approximate. The size of the step in SD is the more useful number.
